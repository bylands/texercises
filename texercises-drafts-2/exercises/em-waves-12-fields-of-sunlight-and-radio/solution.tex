\begin{abcliste}
\abc The intensity is $I = \frac{1}{2}c\,\varepsilon_0\hat E^2$ (the field oscillates; on average, $E^2$ is half of $\hat E^2$):
\begin{align}
 \hat E &= \EsF \\
   &= \sqrt{\frac{2\times\Is}{\ncc\times\nceps}} \\
   &= \Es \approx \result{\EsP{0}{2}}
\end{align}
The two fields of the wave are linked by $\hat E = c\,\hat B$:
\begin{align}
 \hat B &= \BsF = \frac{\Es}{\ncc} = \Bs \approx \result{\BsP{-6}{2}}
\end{align}
A tenth of the Earth's magnetic field.

\abc The same with the intensity of the radio signal:
\begin{align}
 \hat E &= \ErF \\
   &= \sqrt{\frac{2\times\Ir}{\ncc\times\nceps}} \\
   &= \Er \approx \result{\ErP{-3}{2}}
\end{align}
\begin{align}
 \hat B &= \BrF = \frac{\Er}{\ncc} = \Br \approx \result{\BrP{-9}{2}}
\end{align}
Fields this weak are enough for a radio receiver.
\end{abcliste}
