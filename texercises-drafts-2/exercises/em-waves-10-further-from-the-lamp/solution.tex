\begin{abcliste}
\abc The same power spreads over a sphere of area $4\pi r^2$: the intensity falls with $1/r^2$. At three times the distance, the light is spread over nine times the area:
\begin{align}
 I_2 &= \IbF \\
   &= \Ia\times\left(\frac{\ra}{\rb}\right)^2 \\
   &= \Ib \approx \result{\IbP{0}{2}}
\end{align}
The intensity goes with the square of the field, $I = \frac{1}{2}c\,\varepsilon_0\hat E^2$, so the field goes with $\sqrt{I}$, i.e.\ with $1/r$:
\begin{align}
 \frac{\hat E_2}{\hat E_1} &= \sqrt{\frac{I_2}{I_1}} = \ebF = \frac{\ra}{\rb} = \eb \approx \result{\ebP{0}{2}} = \frac{1}{3}
\end{align}

\abc At half the distance the light is spread over a quarter of the area:
\begin{align}
 I_3 &= \IcF \\
   &= \Ia\times\left(\frac{\ra}{\rc}\right)^2 \\
   &= \Ic \approx \result{\IcP{0}{3}}
\end{align}
\begin{align}
 \frac{\hat E_3}{\hat E_1} &= \ecF = \frac{\ra}{\rc} = \result{\ecP{0}{2}}
\end{align}
\end{abcliste}
