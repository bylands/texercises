\begin{abcliste}
\abc In the time $t_1$ the light covers the distance twice, there and back: $2d = c\cdot t_1$.
\begin{align}
 d &= \dmF \\
   &= \frac{\ncc\times\tm}{2} \\
   &= \dm \approx \result{\dmP{0}{3}}
\end{align}
The distance changes over a month between about \SI{356000}{\km} and \SI{407000}{\km}: the Moon's orbit is an ellipse.

\abc The same for the radar echo:
\begin{align}
 d &= \drF \\
   &= \frac{\ncc\times\tp}{2} \\
   &= \dr \approx \result{\drP{3}{2}}
\end{align}
Taking the whole time $t$ would give twice the distance: the signal goes there and comes back.
\end{abcliste}
