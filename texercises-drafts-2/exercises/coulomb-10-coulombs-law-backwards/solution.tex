\begin{abcliste}
\abc Let $q_1$, $q_2$ be the charges, $F$ the force and $r$ the distance. Coulomb's law $F = k\,\frac{|q_1|\,|q_2|}{r^2}$ solved for $r$ gives
\begin{align}
 r &= \raF \\
   &= \sqrt{\frac{\kC\times\qa\times\qb}{\Fa}} \\
   &= \ra \approx \result{\raP{-2}{2}}
\end{align}
(Without the square root one gets $r^2$, not $r$.)

\abc Now $q_1$ is the known charge and $r$ the distance. Solved for the unknown charge:
\begin{align}
 |q_2| &= \qxF \\
   &= \frac{\Fb\times\left(\rb\right)^2}{\kC\times\qc} \\
   &= \qx \approx \qxP{-6}{2}
\end{align}
The spheres repel each other, so the charges have the same sign: the second sphere carries $q_2 = \result{+\qxP{-6}{2}}$.
\end{abcliste}
