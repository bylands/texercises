\abc
\begin{align}
 E &= \EphF = \frac{\nch\times\ncc}{\li} = \Eph \approx \result{\EphP{0}{2}}
\end{align}
In electronvolts, divided by $e = \nce$: $E = \Ephv \approx \result{\EphvP{0}{2}}$.

\abc The power is the energy per second. The number of photons per second is the power divided by the energy of one photon, in joules:
\begin{align}
 \frac{N}{t} &= \frac{P}{E} = \NrF \\
   &= \frac{\Pe\times\li}{\nch\times\ncc} \\
   &= \Nr \approx \result{\NrP{0}{2}}
\end{align}
Even a weak light consists of an enormous number of photons: this is why we do not notice that light comes in portions.
