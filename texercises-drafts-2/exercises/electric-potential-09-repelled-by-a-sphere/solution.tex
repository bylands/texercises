\begin{abcliste}
\abc Outside, a charged sphere acts like a point charge at its centre, so the potential at its surface is $V = k\,\frac{Q}{R}$:
\begin{align}
 \frac{V_B}{V_A} &= \frac{k\cdot 4Q/(2R)}{k\cdot Q/R} = \result{2}
\end{align}
(Not $4/2^2$: that is how the \emph{field} $k\,Q/R^2$ would change.)

\abc Far away the potential is zero: the potential energy $q\,V$ at the surface has become kinetic energy, $E_\mathrm{kin} = q\,V$. The alpha particle has twice the charge and starts at twice the potential:
\begin{align}
 \frac{E_B}{E_A} &= \frac{2e\cdot V_B}{e\cdot V_A} = 2\cdot 2 = \result{4}
\end{align}
The mass does not matter for the energy.

\abc From $\frac{1}{2} m v^2 = E_\mathrm{kin}$ we get $v = \sqrt{2E_\mathrm{kin}/m}$. The energy is four times as large, but so is the mass:
\begin{align}
 \frac{v_B}{v_A} &= \sqrt{\frac{E_B/m_B}{E_A/m_A}} = \sqrt{\frac{4}{4}} = \result{1}
\end{align}
Both particles fly away at the same speed.
\end{abcliste}
