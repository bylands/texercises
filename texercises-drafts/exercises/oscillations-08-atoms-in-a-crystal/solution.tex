From $y = A\cos(\omega t)$ we get $\hat v = A\,\omega$ and $\hat a = A\,\omega^2$, with
\begin{align}
 \omega &= \omF = 2\pi\times\f = \om
\end{align}
The largest speed (at the equilibrium position) is
\begin{align}
 \hat v &= \vmaxF \\
   &= \A\times\om \\
   &= \vmax \approx \result{\vmaxP{0}{2}}
\end{align}
and the largest acceleration (at the turning points)
\begin{align}
 \hat a &= \amaxF \\
   &= \A\times\left(\om\right)^2 \\
   &= \amax \approx \result{\amaxP{15}{2}}
\end{align}
Speeds of a few hundred metres per second, like those of the molecules in the air, and accelerations of about $10^{16}\,\si{\mpss}$.
