\begin{abcliste}
\abc
\begin{align}
 E_\text{UV} &= \EaF = \frac{\nch\times\ncc}{\la} = \Ea \approx \result{\EaP{0}{2}}
\end{align}
This is more than \EcO.

\abc
\begin{align}
 E_\text{IR} &= \EbF = \frac{\nch\times\ncc}{\lb} = \Eb \approx \result{\EbP{0}{2}}
\end{align}
This is far less than \EcO.

\abc Each molecule takes the energy of one photon. $\result{\text{No single infrared photon has enough energy}}$ to change a molecule; more of them only warm the skin. A brighter heater gives more photons, but each one still has too little energy. As in the photoelectric effect, the energy of one photon decides, not the brightness.
\end{abcliste}
