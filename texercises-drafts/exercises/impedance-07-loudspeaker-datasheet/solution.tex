\begin{abcliste}
\abc Above the peak the cone hardly moves, and at a few hundred hertz the reactance $2\pi f L$ of the voice coil is still small: only $R$ is left. The flat bottom of the curve, at about \SI{400}{\hertz}, is the resistance of the coil: $R \approx \result{\RO}$.

\abc The cone on its suspension acts like a parallel resonant circuit: it adds a peak to $Z$ at its resonance frequency. The peak lies at $f_s \approx \result{\fsO}$.

\abc At \faO, $Z \approx \ZaO$. Resistance and reactance add like the sides of a right triangle:
\begin{align}
 X &= \XLF = \sqrt{\left(\ZaO\right)^2 - \left(\RO\right)^2} = \XL \\
 L &= \LvF \\
   &= \frac{\XL}{2\pi\times\fa} \\
   &= \Lv \approx \result{\LvP{-3}{2}}
\end{align}
Over most of the audio range $Z$ lies close to $R$, a little above it; the nominal \SI{8}{\ohm} is a rounded value for that, which amplifiers are built for. An ohmmeter would show only $R$.
\end{abcliste}
