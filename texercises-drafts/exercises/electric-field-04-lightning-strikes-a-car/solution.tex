\begin{abcliste}
\abc The metal body is a closed conductor: the charges stay on its outside and flow round it into the ground. It is \emph{the metal body that shields them}, not the tyres: a few centimetres of rubber cannot stop lightning that has just crossed a kilometre of air.

\abc In a conductor the charges move until there is no field inside: the field inside the closed metal shell is \emph{practically zero} (a Faraday cage).

\abc No: without a closed metal shell the field is not shielded. A convertible with a fabric roof \emph{protects much less}.
\end{abcliste}
