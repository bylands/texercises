\begin{abcliste}
\abc There is no force on a charge moving along the field. Near the equator the field lines run parallel to the ground: particles coming from above move across them and are deflected. Near the poles the field lines point into the ground: particles coming down along them feel hardly any force. The shielding is therefore weakest $\result{\text{near the poles}}$, because there the particles come in along the field lines (even though the field is in fact strongest there).

\abc Right-hand rule for the positive proton: thumb down (the velocity), index finger north (the field), the middle finger (the force) points $\result{\text{east}}$.
\end{abcliste}
