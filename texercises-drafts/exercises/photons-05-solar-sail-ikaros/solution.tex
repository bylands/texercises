\begin{abcliste}
\abc The power of the light falling on the sail is
\begin{align}
 P &= I\cdot a^2 = \PsF = \Is\times\left(\sa\right)^2 = \Ps
\end{align}
Light of power $P$ carries the momentum $P/c$ per second. A mirror reverses the momentum of each photon, so the momentum transferred per second (the force) is twice as large:
\begin{align}
 F &= \frac{2P}{c} = \FsF \\
   &= \frac{2\times\Ps}{\ncc} \\
   &= \Fs \approx \result{\FsP{-3}{2}}
\end{align}
About the weight of a fifth of a gram.

\abc
\begin{align}
 a_\text{probe} &= \frac{F}{m} = \acF = \frac{\Fs}{\mi} = \ac \approx \result{\acP{0}{2}}
\end{align}

\abc
\begin{align}
 \Delta v &= a_\text{probe}\cdot t = \dvsF \\
   &= \ac\times\td \\
   &= \dvs \approx \result{\dvsP{0}{2}}
\end{align}
without any fuel.
\end{abcliste}
