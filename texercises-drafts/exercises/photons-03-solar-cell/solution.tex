\begin{abcliste}
\abc At the threshold, the photon energy equals the band gap, $\frac{hc}{\lambda_G} = E_G$:
\begin{align}
 \lambda_G &= \lgrF = \frac{\nch\times\ncc}{\EgO} = \lgr \approx \result{\lgrP{-6}{2}}
\end{align}
in the near infrared.

\abc Each photon of \lirO\ has only
\begin{align}
 E &= \EirF = \Eir \approx \EirP{0}{2}
\end{align}
less than the band gap (\lirO\ is longer than $\lambda_G$). It produces $\result{\text{no current}}$, however bright the light is: each electron needs one photon with at least the band gap energy, more weak photons do not help.

\abc A photon of \laO\ has
\begin{align}
 E &= \EaF = \Ea \approx \EaP{0}{2}
\end{align}
Only \EgO\ of it are used; the part that turns into heat is
\begin{align}
 \frac{E-E_G}{E} &= \frac{\Ea-\Eg}{\Ea} = \loss \approx \result{\losspP{0}{2}}
\end{align}
These two losses are the main reason why a silicon cell turns at most about a third of the sunlight into electric energy.
\end{abcliste}
