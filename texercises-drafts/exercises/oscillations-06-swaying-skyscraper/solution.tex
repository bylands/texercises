From $y = A\cos(\omega t)$ we get $\hat v = A\,\omega$ and $\hat a = A\,\omega^2$, with
\begin{align}
 \omega &= \omF = \frac{2\pi}{\T} = \om
\end{align}
The largest speed is reached at the equilibrium position:
\begin{align}
 \hat v &= \vmaxF \\
   &= \A\times\om \\
   &= \vmax \approx \result{\vmaxP{-2}{2}}
\end{align}
The largest acceleration is reached at the turning points:
\begin{align}
 \hat a &= \amaxF \\
   &= \A\times\left(\om\right)^2 \\
   &= \amax \approx \result{\amaxP{0}{2}}
\end{align}
Clearly more than people can feel (\afO): that is why such towers carry huge pendulums that damp the swaying.
