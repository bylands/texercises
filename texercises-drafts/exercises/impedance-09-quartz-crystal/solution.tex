\begin{abcliste}
\abc The minimum is the series resonance $f_s$ of $L_1$ and $C_1$; the maximum, a little higher, is the parallel resonance $f_p$, where $C_0$ joins in. From the graph, $f_p - f_s \approx \result{\dfO}$.

\abc Solving $f_p - f_s = f_s\,C_1/(2C_0)$ for $C_1$:
\begin{align}
 C_1 &= \CaF \\
   &= \frac{2\times\Cz\times\df}{\fs} \\
   &= \Ca \approx \result{\CaP{-15}{2}}
\end{align}

\abc $L_1$ and $C_1$ have the series resonance $f_s$, $2\pi f_s = 1/\sqrt{L_1 C_1}$:
\begin{align}
 L_1 &= \LaF \\
   &= \frac{1}{\left(2\pi\times\fs\right)^2\times\Ca} \\
   &= \La \approx \result{\LaP{0}{2}}
\end{align}
A tenth of a henry and a few femtofarads: no coil and capacitor could be built like that. Because the vibration loses so little energy, the resonance is extremely sharp, and the clock is precise to a few seconds a month.
\end{abcliste}
