\begin{abcliste}
\abc Outside, the sphere acts like a point charge $Q$ at its centre. At its surface,
\begin{align}
 E = k\,\frac{Q}{R^2}, \qquad V = k\,\frac{Q}{R} \qquad\Rightarrow\qquad E = \frac{V}{R}
\end{align}
Sparks start when the field at the surface reaches the breakdown field $E_\mathrm{b}$:
\begin{align}
 V_\mathrm{max} &= \VF \\
   &= \Eb\times\Rs \\
   &= \result{\VP{3}{2}}
\end{align}

\abc From $V = k\,Q/R$:
\begin{align}
 Q &= \QF \\
   &= \frac{\V\times\Rs}{\nckc} \\
   &= \Q \approx \result{\QP{-6}{2}}
\end{align}

\abc $V_\mathrm{max} = E_\mathrm{b}\,R$: a larger sphere reaches a \emph{higher voltage}. Sharp points do the opposite: there the field is strong already at low voltage, and sparks start early.
\end{abcliste}
