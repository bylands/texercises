\begin{circuitikz}[european resistors]
 \draw (0,0) to[sV, l={$V = \VO$}] (0,2.5)
   to[R, l={$R_\mathrm{B} = \RbO$}] (3.5,2.5)
   to[R, l={$R_\mathrm{S}$}] (7,2.5)
   to[short, i={$I$}] (7,0) -- (0,0);
\end{circuitikz}
\end{center}

Body and shoes (with the ground) are in series: their resistances $R_\mathrm{B}$ and $R_\mathrm{S}$ add up.

\begin{abcliste}
\abc With dry shoes,
\begin{align}
 I_1 &= \IaF \\
   &= \frac{\V}{\Rb + \Rs} \\
   &= \result{\IaP{-3}{2}}
\end{align}

\abc Barefoot on wet grass, with the much smaller resistance of the wet ground,
\begin{align}
 I_2 &= \IbF \\
   &= \frac{\V}{\Rb + \Rw} \\
   &= \result{\IbP{-3}{2}}
\end{align}
The current is $\ratioF = \ratioP{0}{2}$ times larger, and deadly: dry, insulating shoes protect.
\end{abcliste}
