\begin{abcliste}
\abc The magnetic force is the centripetal force:
\begin{align}
 e v B &= \frac{m v^2}{r} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad r = \frac{m v}{e B} = \frac{p}{e B}
\end{align}
The momentum is therefore
\begin{align}
 p &= \pF \\
   &= \nce\times\B\times\r \\
   &= \p \approx \result{\pP{0}{2}}
\end{align}

\abc Since $r = \frac{p}{eB}$, the larger the momentum, the larger the radius and the straighter the track: the particle has a $\result{\text{very large momentum}}$. (A neutral particle would leave no track at all in such a detector.)

\abc The direction of the bending depends only on the sign of the charge (right-hand rule): their charges have $\result{\text{opposite signs}}$. Different masses or speeds change only the radius.
\end{abcliste}
