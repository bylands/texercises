\begin{abcliste}
\abc The peak lies at $f_0 \approx \result{\fzO}$.

\abc Coil and capacitance form a parallel resonant circuit; $R$ is small compared with $2\pi f_0 L$, so the peak lies at the resonance frequency:
\begin{align}
 2\pi f_0 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{L C}} \quad\Rightarrow\quad C &= \CF \\
   &= \frac{1}{\left(2\pi\times\fz\right)^2\times\Lp} \\
   &= \C \approx \result{\CP{-12}{2}}
\end{align}

\abc Without the $C_\mathrm{w}$ of the pickup, the cable has $C - C_\mathrm{w}$:
\begin{align}
 \ell &= \lcF \\
   &= \frac{\C - \Cw}{\cp} \\
   &= \lc \approx \result{\lcP{0}{2}}
\end{align}
The peak boosts the frequencies around $f_0$ and gives the guitar its bright sound. A longer cable means more $C$ and a lower peak: the sound gets duller. Guitarists hear the difference between a \SI{3}{\meter} and a \SI{10}{\meter} cable.
\end{abcliste}
