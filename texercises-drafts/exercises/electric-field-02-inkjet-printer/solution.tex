\begin{abcliste}
\abc Like a ball thrown horizontally: constant speed $v$ along the plates, constant acceleration $a = q E/m$ across. The drop is between the plates for the time $t = L/v$, so
\begin{align}
 y &= \frac{1}{2}\,a\,t^2 = \frac{1}{2}\,\frac{q E}{m}\left(\frac{L}{v}\right)^2 = \yF \\
   &= \frac{\q\times\E\times\left(\Lp\right)^2}{2\times\m\times\left(\vd\right)^2} \\
   &= \y \approx \result{\yP{-3}{2}}
\end{align}

\abc The drops are negative: they are pulled \emph{towards the positive plate}, against the direction of the field.

\abc A drop without charge feels no electric force: it \emph{flies straight on} (and is caught by a gutter).
\end{abcliste}
