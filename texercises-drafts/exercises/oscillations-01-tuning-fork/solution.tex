From $y = A\cos(\omega t)$ we get $\hat v = A\,\omega$ and $\hat a = A\,\omega^2$. The angular frequency is
\begin{align}
 \omega &= \omF = 2\pi\times\f = \om
\end{align}

\begin{abcliste}
\abc
The largest speed is reached at the equilibrium position:
\begin{align}
 \hat v &= \vmaxF \\
   &= \A\times\om \\
   &= \vmax \approx \result{\vmaxP{0}{2}}
\end{align}
The largest acceleration is reached at the turning points:
\begin{align}
 \hat a &= \amaxF \\
   &= \A\times\left(\om\right)^2 \\
   &= \amax \approx \result{\amaxP{0}{2}}
\end{align}

\abc
\begin{align}
 \frac{\hat a}{g} &= \frac{\amax}{\ncg} = \rg \approx \result{\rgP{0}{2}}
\end{align}
Hundreds of $g$, with a speed of less than \SI{1}{\mps}: at high frequencies, small oscillations mean huge accelerations.
\end{abcliste}
