\begin{abcliste}
\abc The echo arrives $\Delta t = \dtO$ after the pulse. In that time the pulse ran to the fault and back:
\begin{align}
 d &= \dF = \frac{\vs\times\dt}{2} = \d \approx \result{\dP{0}{2}}
\end{align}

\abc An open end lets the voltage at the end of the cable swing freely, like a free end: the echo would come back upright. A short circuit holds the voltage at 0, like a fixed end: the echo comes back upside down. Here the echo is upside down: it is a $\result{\text{short circuit}}$.

Cable testers (time-domain reflectometers) find faults this way, to within a metre.
\end{abcliste}
